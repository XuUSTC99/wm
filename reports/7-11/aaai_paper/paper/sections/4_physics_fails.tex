\section{Physical Inductive Biases Fail}
\label{sec:fails}

We inject physical structure into the shared $192$-dimensional latent through five mechanisms spanning the design space---from pinning \emph{what the state is} to constraining \emph{how it evolves}, with and without labels (Table~\ref{tab:mechanisms}). All variants run on top of free rollout, i.e., they must beat an already strong training protocol, which is the deployment-relevant question.

\subsection{Post-hoc Injection: Every Mechanism Hurts}
\label{sec:posthoc}

Table~\ref{tab:injection} reports the hardest clean-split latent nMSE for each mechanism against the pure free-rollout baseline---$30$ mechanism--domain cells in total. Three observations stand out. First, \emph{correct physics does not help}: on parabola, the strict gravitational head---the ground-truth functional form, with only the gravity constant learned---degrades error from $0.127$ to $0.173$, no better than an unconstrained MLP head ($0.178$). Second, \emph{labels do not help}: the label-free prior degrades all three domains (collision most, $0.393\!\to\!0.653$), and so does its fully grounded counterpart with perfect position labels ($0.131\!\to\!0.166$ on uniform; $0.393\!\to\!0.525$ on collision). Third, \emph{combinations do not rescue}: probe supervision alone is harmful ($0.131\!\to\!0.167$ on uniform, confirmed in pixel space, $20.41\!\to\!19.83$\,dB; $0.393\!\to\!0.647$ on collision), and adding it to the reweighted slot yields parity at best ($0.125$ vs.\ $0.132\pm0.017$ on uniform; $0.127$ on parabola) or clear damage ($0.607$ on collision), while its pixel rollout falls below even the baseline ($20.02$ vs.\ $20.41$\,dB). The form-free consistency loss, designed specifically for collision impulses, fails worst exactly there ($0.57$--$0.64$ vs.\ $0.393$), and fails on the smooth domains too ($0.151$ vs.\ $0.131$; $0.147$ vs.\ $0.127$).

Across the grid, $29$ of $30$ cells fail to improve on the baseline. One apparent exception is pure seed noise: probe-only parabola reads $0.115$, exactly the bottom of the baseline's own seed range ($0.115$--$0.127$). The other is real but domain-bound: extending the load-bearing slot to carry velocity as well as position helps on parabola (\rmood{} $0.122{\pm}0.007\!\to\!0.096{\pm}0.007$ over three seeds, non-overlapping intervals, every seed lower), yet the very same encoding is the \emph{worst} variant on uniform ($0.114\!\to\!0.207$) and clearly harmful on collision ($0.621$). The sign is set by whether velocity is informative in that domain's dynamics: it linearly drives the parabola trajectory, is a redundant constant under uniform motion, and jumps discontinuously at collision. This domain-dependence is the finding, not a coincidence to be tuned away: a fixed physical form can match only a subset of dynamics, so no fixed prior is portable across domains---the same lesson the exact gravitational form $a{=}g$ teaches, textbook-correct on parabola yet still degrading it ($0.127\!\to\!0.173$, Table~\ref{tab:injection}). The pattern is systematic in \emph{what} is encoded. Pinning position---an output variable, identical in role across domains---degrades monotonically with dynamics complexity (uniform parity, parabola then collision harm), never flipping sign; adding velocity, a driving variable whose role differs by domain, flips it, and its marginal effect ($+0.075/{-}0.064/{+}0.025$ nMSE on uniform/parabola/collision; App.~\ref{app:injection}) tracks precisely whether velocity is redundant, driving, or discontinuous there. Even the one favorable landing is worth just $-0.026$ nMSE, against the $2.2$--$3.6\times$ of free rollout (\S\ref{sec:fr}).

\subsection{From-Scratch Co-Training Hurts More}
\label{sec:scratch}

A natural defense holds that physical structure cannot be grafted onto an encoder already committed to a black-box representation---it must be baked in during pretraining, as extrinsic architectures do. We test this with a $2{\times}2$ factorial (init $\in$ \{PushT post-hoc, from-scratch\} $\times$ physics $\in$ \{off, on\}) per domain, with learning rate re-tuned so the from-scratch baselines converge cleanly. The defense fails, and in the strongest possible way (Table~\ref{tab:scratch}): from-scratch physics is \emph{more} damaging than post-hoc grafting. On uniform motion, the physics penalty grows from $+0.035$ (post-hoc) to $+0.558$ (from-scratch)---the cleaner the baseline, the larger the harm. All three domains show positive penalties in both regimes; the ``inject at pretraining'' escape hatch is closed.

\subsection{The Load-Bearing Problem}
\label{sec:lbr}

Why does structure fail so uniformly? The physical slot occupies $2$ of $192$ dimensions; under a mean prediction loss it receives ${\sim}1\%$ of the gradient, so the predictor pays almost nothing for getting it wrong. Meanwhile the black-box channel \emph{redundantly encodes the same position}, opening a bypass. We verify the bypass directly: probing \emph{only the $190$ non-slot dimensions} of a slot-pinned model still decodes position at $\rho{=}0.79$--$0.92$ across all three domains---as well as the full latent, and undiminished by pinning the slot (which faithfully absorbs position, its supervision loss falling $2.53\!\to\!0.015$; probing the two slot dimensions alone recovers it). Position is thus a redundant, distributed copy the predictor can route through while ignoring the structured slot entirely: the slot is decodable but not load-bearing. Worse, the auxiliary gradients actively compete with prediction: at probe weight $\lambda{=}10$ the probe-to-prediction gradient ratio reaches $15$--$125\times$ across domains and the encoder's effective dimensionality collapses by $39$--$90\%$.

This account makes a falsifiable prediction: re-weighting the prediction loss so the slot dimensions carry weight (load-bearing reweighting, LBR; weight ${\sim}30$ on the slot) should remove the damage. On uniform motion it does (Table~\ref{tab:lbr}): the harmful fixed slot ($0.183$) returns exactly to baseline parity ($0.132\pm0.017$ vs.\ $0.136\pm0.009$ over three seeds)---and no further. The rescue is domain-limited: on parabola and collision even the reweighted slot remains harmful ($0.160$ vs.\ $0.127$; $0.596$ vs.\ $0.393$), where the slot's fixed content competes with richer dynamics. The surviving benefits are narrow and metric-dependent: with a kinematic head on the now load-bearing slot, velocity-OOD decoded position reaches $\rho{=}0.99$ and long-horizon pixel accuracy improves by $+1.25$\,dB on uniform (single-seed), only in smooth domains, and at the cost of degraded zero-shot transfer ($0.551$, the worst of all variants). Reweighting removes the slot's own harm but not the black box's redundant copy of position: the bypass survives, and with it the failure of every injection that leaves the bypass intact. Structure, even when made load-bearing, does not buy \emph{portable} net robustness---the one seed-robust gain in our grid (\S\ref{sec:posthoc}) is confined to the single domain whose dynamics it matches and reverses elsewhere---consistent with attributing the reported success of extrinsic architectures \citep{mao2024piwm} to their \emph{architecture} (physical state as the sole, unavoidable prediction pathway) rather than to physical equations.
