\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We asked whether injecting physics can make a latent world model physically consistent, and answered with a controlled anatomy: injection fails in $29$ of $30$ mechanism--domain cells---with the exact gravitational form, with perfect labels, and whether grafted onto a pretrained encoder or co-trained from scratch. The account we defend is that the state is already there: the non-slot dimensions carry the same position ($\rho=0.79$--$0.92$), so an injected copy adds no signal while the constraint consumes capacity and gradient---\emph{load-bearing but redundant}. Intervention settles the half we could previously only infer, and settles it against our first reading: the injected slot \emph{is} read, by three methods that fail in different ways, while the black box keeps its own copy of the same state. Both of the account's predictions held: re-weighting the slot reaches only parity, and an architecture that removes the redundant route does help, until an OOD shift reaches its encoder. Removing that route is necessary, not sufficient. What moved prediction was never the structure but the training protocol, worth $2.2$--$8.3\times$ on identical code and metrics.

The constructive reading: an inductive bias must supply what the latent is missing---\emph{how the state evolves, not what the state is}. Two routes stay open: injections that target dynamics or conserved quantities rather than state, and extrinsic designs paired with OOD-robust encoders. Our own evolution-targeting variants are deliberately simple: a second-order kinematic update and a finite-difference consistency prior, written onto slot coordinates. Their failure shows that pinning the evolution this way is not \emph{automatically} useful---not that evolution injection fails in general. Collision makes the gap concrete: it turns on contact, impulse and inter-object relation, none of which a smoothness prior over one object's positions can express, so our collision result bounds that prior rather than the operator-level designs (symplectic, Hamiltonian, learned contact) that would represent it properly.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Our training-dynamics conclusions rest primarily on one compact backbone (${\sim}10$M ViT-tiny JEPA); the headline findings replicate on a frozen-DINOv2 backbone (\S\ref{sec:externalres}), where injection is inert rather than harmful---so the \emph{harm} claim is established on the trainable-encoder regime. All $30$ scan cells, the re-weighting sweep, and the from-scratch $2{\times}2$ are three-seed; the Physion++ variants remain single-seed. The probe variants remain untested on Physion++, and the one favorable cell (velocity on parabola) suggests dynamics-matched injection deserves study we did not perform. On the mechanism, the half we can now demonstrate is that the injected slot is read; the half that stays partly inferential is the black box's own causal role. Amnesic projection, the standard tool for that question, cannot answer it here: erasing position linearly requires removing $116$--$154$ of the $190$ non-slot dimensions, and at that rank removing position is indistinguishable from removing the same number of dimensions at random---a direct consequence of how distributed the copy is, and a limit we quantify rather than a step we skipped. Wholesale replacement of the black box does establish its causal role, but its controls pass in only one of six backbone--domain cells. So ``both channels carry the same state'' rests on measurement for the slot and on convergent but incomplete evidence for the black box.
