\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We asked whether injecting physics can make a latent world model physically consistent, and answered with a controlled anatomy: injection fails in $26$ of $27$ cells---with the exact gravitational form, with perfect labels, grafted on or co-trained from scratch. The account we defend is that the state is already there: the non-slot dimensions carry the same position, so an injected copy adds no signal while the constraint consumes capacity and gradient---\emph{load-bearing but redundant}. Intervention settles the half that decodability alone leaves open, and against the reading Tests 1--3 invite: the injected slot \emph{is} read, by three methods that fail in different ways, while the black box keeps its own copy. Re-weighting reaches only parity; an architecture that removes the redundancy does help, until an OOD shift reaches its encoder---not, by itself, a fix. What moved prediction was never the structure but the training protocol, worth $2.2$--$8.3\times$ on identical code and metrics.

The constructive reading: an inductive bias must supply what the latent is missing---\emph{how the state evolves, not what the state is}. Two routes stay open: injections that target dynamics or conserved quantities rather than state, and extrinsic designs paired with OOD-robust encoders. Our own evolution-targeting variants are deliberately simple---a kinematic update and a consistency prior on slot coordinates---so their failure bounds that class, not evolution injection in general: collision turns on contact and inter-object relation, which only operator-level designs (symplectic, learned contact) can express.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Our conclusions rest mainly on one compact backbone (${\sim}10$M ViT-tiny JEPA); the primary findings replicate on frozen DINOv2, where injection is largely inert rather than harmful---so the \emph{harm} claim is established on the trainable-encoder regime. On the mechanism, the slot's use is demonstrated; the black box's role stays partly inferential---erasing its position copy takes $116$--$154$ of $190$ dimensions, and at that rank no valid same-rank control exists---a rank-matched random projection removes $2.9$--$4.7\times$ more of the black box's variance than the position ablation does, so the comparison reports how much each removed rather than which information, and matching the variance is geometrically impossible there (a limit we measure, not a step we skipped)---while wholesale replacement passes its controls in one of six cells.
