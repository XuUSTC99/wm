\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We asked whether injecting physics can make a latent world model physically consistent, and answered with a controlled anatomy: injection fails in $29$ of $30$ mechanism--domain cells---with the exact gravitational form, with perfect labels, and whether grafted onto a pretrained encoder or co-trained from scratch. The account we defend is that the state is already there: the non-slot dimensions carry the same position ($\rho=0.79$--$0.92$), so an injected copy adds no signal while the constraint consumes capacity and gradient---\emph{decodable but not load-bearing}. All three tests fit it, and both of its predictions held: re-weighting the slot reaches only parity, and an architecture that removes the redundant route does help, until an OOD shift reaches its encoder. Removing that route is necessary, not sufficient. What moved prediction was never the structure but the training protocol, worth $2.2$--$8.3\times$ on identical code and metrics.

The constructive reading: an inductive bias must supply what the latent is missing---\emph{how the state evolves, not what the state is}. Two routes stay open: injections that target dynamics or conserved quantities rather than state, and extrinsic designs paired with OOD-robust encoders. Our own evolution-targeting variants are deliberately simple: a second-order kinematic update and a finite-difference consistency prior, written onto slot coordinates. Their failure shows that pinning the evolution this way is not \emph{automatically} useful---not that evolution injection fails in general. Collision makes the gap concrete: it turns on contact, impulse and inter-object relation, none of which a smoothness prior over one object's positions can express, so our collision result bounds that prior rather than the operator-level designs (symplectic, Hamiltonian, learned contact) that would represent it properly.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Our training-dynamics conclusions rest primarily on one compact backbone (${\sim}10$M ViT-tiny JEPA); the headline findings replicate on a frozen-DINOv2 backbone (\S\ref{sec:externalres}), where injection is inert rather than harmful---so the \emph{harm} claim is established on the trainable-encoder regime. Headline comparisons, re-weighting, and the seed-refuted cells are three-seed; the remaining $26$ scan cells and the Physion++ variants are single-seed, with effects $5$--$20$ seed-std and $3$--$10\times$ clear of the noise band. The probe variants remain untested on Physion++, and the one favorable cell (velocity on parabola) suggests dynamics-matched injection deserves study we did not perform. Most importantly, our mechanism is inferential where it is strongest: we measure that the redundant route exists, that the constraint acts, and that its strength is not the limit, but we never intervene on a trained model to show prediction \emph{takes} that route. The decisive experiments---amnesic projection of the position subspace out of the non-slot dimensions, counterfactual patching of slot against black box during rollout, and causal mediation over the two channels---remain open, and until they are run ``prediction reads the redundant copy'' is the account that best fits our evidence rather than a demonstrated fact.
