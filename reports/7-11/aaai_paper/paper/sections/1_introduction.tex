\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A \emph{world model} rolls an internal representation forward to predict how an environment evolves under an agent's actions---a simulator to plan with instead of acting in the world \citep{ha2018world,lecun2022path}. For a physical environment, it should respect how objects move, fall, and collide. JEPA-style latent world models \citep{maes2025lewm,balestriero2025lejepa} present a puzzle here. Trained on PhyWorld \citep{kang2024how} with no physics supervision, such a model already recovers object position from its representation by linear probing (correlation up to $0.96$) and predicts one step almost exactly (latent cosine $0.98$--$0.99$)---yet rolled out autoregressively, its predictions drift away from the very dynamics it encodes: on collision the latent cosine falls to $0.24$ by horizon $28$, and out-of-distribution (OOD) physics---unseen radii, masses, or velocities---multiplies rollout error several-fold. This indicates the state is already present in the representation; the model simply fails to \emph{evolve} it.

The popular fix is to \emph{inject physical structure into the latent} (Figure~\ref{fig:arch}): a dedicated slot pinned to the physical state, a supervised probe that reads it out, or a hard-wired dynamics rule. Such injection has real successes in adjacent model classes---architectures that route prediction through a low-dimensional physical state by construction \citep{mao2024piwm,peper2025principles}, deep supervision where the supervised quantities occupy a large fraction of a compact state latent \citep{zahorodnii2025deepsup}, and a decomposed JEPA latent supervised on low-dimensional numerical time series \citep{nie2026physjepa}. Scaling data is not the answer either: video generators trained at scale still fail OOD physics, memorizing cases rather than laws \citep{kang2024how}. But for a \emph{shared, high-dimensional} latent---where prediction runs end-to-end through one representation---injection has only been tried in isolated, incomparable settings, and never against the confound that dominates the outcome: the training protocol. Whether physical structure adds anything, once the protocol is done right, has never been tested systematically.

We answer this with a systematic, controlled anatomy on a JEPA world model. We cross ten injection variants---spanning hard and soft constraints, targeting the physical \emph{state} (what it is) or its \emph{evolution} (how it changes), under labeled and label-free supervision---with two training regimes and three controlled physics domains, then corroborate on two realistic-simulation benchmarks \citep{bear2021physion,tung2023physionpp}.

Across the full grid, the structure does not help: injection degrades or fails to improve prediction in $29$ of $30$ mechanism--domain cells. Supplying the \emph{correct} evolution rule---the gravitational law $a{=}g$ wired into the state update---does no better than a freely learned acceleration where $a{=}g$ \emph{is} the ground truth ($0.151$ vs.\ $0.149$ nMSE on parabola), and degrades uniform motion by $1.27\times$. Co-training the structure from scratch, rather than grafting it onto a pretrained encoder, does not rescue it either: over three seeds it remains harmful on uniform ($+0.435$ nMSE, against $+0.037$ fine-tuned) and on collision, and reaches only parity on parabola---never help---closing the defense that physics must be baked in during pretraining. The one cell that does improve reverses sign on the other two domains: using it would mean knowing each domain's dynamics in advance.

Why should the \emph{correct} physics hurt? Because the physical state is \emph{already there}. A pinned slot occupies $2$ of the $192$ latent dimensions, but the other $190$ redundantly encode the same position---decoding it nearly as accurately as the full latent---so an injected copy adds no information the representation lacks, while the constraint still competes with prediction for capacity and gradient. Redundancy accounts for the absent gain, competition for the harm. What injection adds is not a route the prediction lacked but a \emph{second} route to a state it already had, and intervening on a trained model shows prediction takes both: steering the slot moves the model's own black-box state by $1.5$--$2.0\times$ while a norm-matched random direction moves it ${\approx}0$, and swapping the slot for another trajectory's makes prediction follow that trajectory $47$--$72\%$ of the way where a model without a slot follows $1.5$--$11\%$. The injected slot is thus \emph{load-bearing but redundant}---used, and carrying nothing the black box was not already carrying. This also explains why strengthening it cannot help: if the slot's ${\sim}1\%$ share of the prediction loss were what starves it, up-weighting should remove the harm, and it does not---re-weighting to saturation recovers only \emph{parity}, never a net gain, because the constraint was never short of gradient, only short of new information.

What the representation lacks is not the state but the ability to \emph{evolve} it over long horizons, and that gap is fixable with no physics at all. Repairing the training protocol alone does it: free rollout, with the horizon matched to each domain's dynamics, cuts rollout error $2.2$--$8.3\times$ on the identical code, metrics, and seeds that judged every injection cell. That pins the failure on the injected structure rather than on the pipeline or the yardstick. The lesson is constructive: an inductive bias must supply what a shared latent is missing (how the state evolves), not re-encode what it already holds. If that reading is right, only an architecture can close the route; no loss weight can. Porting one that makes the physical state the \emph{sole} pathway for prediction \citep{mao2024piwm} does close it, but it collapses once an OOD shift reaches its encoder ($\rho$ $0.33$ vs.\ $0.89$): closing the bypass is necessary, but not sufficient. Every finding above replicates on a second, frozen-DINOv2 backbone.

This paper contributes:
\begin{itemize}
    \item to our knowledge, the first systematic anatomy of physics injection in \emph{shared-latent} visual world models---ten variants $\times$ two training regimes $\times$ three domains, plus two realistic-simulation benchmarks---finding injection no better than baseline in $29$ of $30$ cells, and replicating on a frozen-DINOv2 backbone (\S\ref{sec:scan}, \S\ref{sec:externalres});
    \item a mechanistic account, \emph{load-bearing but redundant}, established by intervention rather than inferred: three independent interventions on trained models---steering, counterfactual patching, and per-dimension Jacobians---agree that the injected slot \emph{is} used, while the $190$ black-box dimensions decode position as accurately as all $192$ and keep doing so once the slot is pinned. Prediction reads both copies of one state, so re-weighting the slot to saturation buys only parity. A faithful extrinsic port supports this from the outside: making the physical state the sole route does help, yet collapses under encoder-OOD shift (\S\ref{sec:mech}, \S\ref{sec:externalres});
    \item a controlled factorial showing the dominant variable is the training protocol, not physical structure: free rollout alone cuts rollout error $2.2$--$8.3\times$ with no physics added, doubling as the positive control against implementation and metric artifacts (\S\ref{sec:fr}).
\end{itemize}
