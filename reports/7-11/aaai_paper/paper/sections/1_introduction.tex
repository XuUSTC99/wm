\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A \emph{world model} rolls an internal representation forward to predict how an environment evolves under an agent's actions---a simulator to plan with instead of acting in the world \citep{ha2018world,lecun2022path}. For a physical environment, it should respect how objects move, fall, and collide. JEPA-style latent world models \citep{maes2025lewm,balestriero2025lejepa} present a puzzle here. Trained on PhyWorld \citep{kang2024how} with no physics supervision, such a model already recovers object position from its representation by linear probing (correlation up to $0.96$) and predicts one step almost exactly (latent cosine $0.98$--$0.99$)---yet rolled out autoregressively, its predictions drift away from the very dynamics it encodes: on collision the latent cosine falls to $0.24$ by horizon $28$, and out-of-distribution (OOD) physics---unseen radii, masses, or velocities---multiplies rollout error several-fold. The state is present in the representation; the model simply fails to \emph{evolve} it.

The popular fix is to \emph{inject physical structure into the latent} (Figure~\ref{fig:arch}): a dedicated slot pinned to the physical state, a supervised probe that reads it out, or a hard-wired dynamics rule. Such injection has real successes in adjacent model classes---architectures that route prediction through a low-dimensional physical state by construction \citep{mao2024piwm,peper2025principles}, and deep supervision in a compact state latent where the supervised quantities occupy a large fraction of the representation \citep{zahorodnii2025deepsup,nie2026physjepa}. Scaling data is not the answer either: video generators trained at scale still fail OOD physics, memorizing cases rather than laws \citep{kang2024how}. But for a \emph{shared, high-dimensional} latent---where prediction runs end-to-end through one representation---injection has only been tried in isolated, incomparable settings, and never against the confound that dominates the outcome: the training protocol. Whether physical structure adds anything, once the protocol is done right, has never been tested systematically.

We answer this with a systematic, controlled anatomy on a compact JEPA world model. We cross ten injection variants---spanning hard and soft constraints, targeting the physical \emph{state} (what it is) or its \emph{evolution} (how it changes), under labeled and label-free supervision---with two training regimes and three controlled physics domains, and validate the surviving conclusions on two realistic-simulation benchmarks \citep{bear2021physion,tung2023physionpp}. Verdicts rest on scale-aware prediction error (latent nMSE and pixel PSNR), never on probe correlation or cosine---diagnostics that rise mechanically with the injected loss and can manufacture apparent gains; all headline comparisons are seed-controlled (\S\ref{sec:setup}).

The structure does not help. Injection degrades or fails to improve prediction in $29$ of $30$ mechanism--domain cells, and the failures are pointed. Supplying the \emph{correct} physical form---the exact gravitational law $a{=}g$---hurts as much as an unconstrained dynamics head ($1.57\times$ the baseline error on uniform motion). Co-training the structure from scratch, rather than grafting it onto a pretrained encoder, hurts \emph{more}, not less ($+0.558$ vs.\ $+0.035$ nMSE)---closing the defense that physics must be baked in during pretraining. The lone seed-robust gain reverses sign across domains: a diagnostic signature, not a usable prior.

Why should the \emph{correct} physics hurt? Because the physical state is \emph{already there}. A pinned slot occupies $2$ of the $192$ latent dimensions, but the other $190$ redundantly encode the same position---decoding it nearly as accurately as the full latent---so prediction routes around the injected slot and reads the copy it already carries. This account makes a falsifiable prediction: if the slot's ${\sim}1\%$ share of the prediction loss is what starves it, up-weighting the slot should remove the harm. It does not---re-weighting to saturation recovers only \emph{parity}, never a net gain, because no loss weight closes a bypass wired into the architecture. The state is \emph{decodable, but not load-bearing}.

What the representation lacks is not the state but the ability to \emph{evolve} it over long horizons---and that gap is fixable with no physics at all. Repairing the training protocol alone---free rollout, with the horizon matched to each domain's dynamics---cuts rollout error $2.2$--$8.3\times$ on the identical code, metrics, and seeds that judged every injection cell, pinning the failure on the injected structure rather than the pipeline or the yardstick. The lesson is constructive: to help a shared-latent world model, an inductive bias must supply what the representation is missing---how the state evolves---not re-encode what it already holds. An architecture that makes the physical state the \emph{sole} pathway prediction can take does close the bypass; but ported faithfully to our benchmark, it collapses once an OOD shift reaches its encoder ($\rho$ $0.33$ vs.\ $0.89$)---necessary, but not sufficient. Every finding above replicates on a second, frozen-DINOv2 backbone.

This paper contributes:
\begin{itemize}
    \item the first systematic, seed-controlled anatomy of physical inductive biases in \emph{visual} latent world models---five mechanism families, ten variants, two training regimes, three synthetic domains, and two realistic-simulation benchmarks---finding injection no better than baseline in $29$ of $30$ cells, and replicating on a frozen-DINOv2 backbone (\S\ref{sec:scan}, \S\ref{sec:externalres});
    \item a mechanistic account, \emph{decodable but not load-bearing}, supported by a black-box bypass probe, a loss-dominance and effective-dimensionality measurement, and a dose--response re-weighting sweep; its architectural prediction is confirmed externally---a faithful extrinsic port closes the bypass, yet collapses under encoder-OOD shift, making extrinsic design necessary but not sufficient (\S\ref{sec:mech}, \S\ref{sec:externalres});
    \item a controlled factorial showing the dominant variable is the training protocol, not physical structure: free rollout alone cuts rollout error $2.2$--$8.3\times$ with no physics added, doubling as the positive control that excludes implementation and metric artifacts (\S\ref{sec:fr}).
\end{itemize}
