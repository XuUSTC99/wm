\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Model.}
The compact, fully controlled backbone of \S\ref{sec:prelim} (LeWM: $E_\theta$, an MLP projector, an action encoder, and a $6$-layer causal-transformer $F_\phi$) allows the complete factorial anatomy (${\sim}200$ training runs), with headline configurations repeated over three seeds ($3072/1234/42$); remaining cells are single-seed and marked as such.

\paragraph{Datasets and OOD protocol.}
All benchmarks are \emph{visual} world-modeling tasks (RGB video frames), not the numerical/tabular sequences of prior physics-injection work (\S\ref{sec:related}); injected state is thus always a small fraction of a high-dimensional visual latent. \emph{PhyWorld} \citep{kang2024how} provides three single-mechanism synthetic domains---\emph{uniform motion}, \emph{parabola}, \emph{collision} (zero/constant/impulsive acceleration)---each with $1{,}000$ ID trajectories ($32$ frames, $224{\times}224$; radius/mass $\in[0.7,1.5]$, velocity $\in[1,4]$), evaluated on four partitions (ID, \rmood{}, \vood{}, \bothood{}); its single-variable OOD ranges isolate physical extrapolation from data-scale confounds. Two \emph{realistic-simulation} benchmarks (rendered, not camera video) add realism: \emph{Physion} \citep{bear2021physion} for zero-shot object-contact-prediction (OCP) transfer, and \emph{Physion++} \citep{tung2023physionpp}---ten physical-property scenarios (mass/friction/bounce/deformation)---for direct training with rollout to horizon $64$.

\paragraph{Metrics and decision rules.}
Verdicts use two scale-aware, \emph{external} metrics: latent nMSE ($\|\hat{\lat}-\lat\|^2$ over target variance) and pixel PSNR of decoded rollouts---external because no injection variant optimizes them directly, and they agree in every case we examined. Latent cosine and probe decodability $\rho$ serve as \emph{diagnostics only}: both are duals of the auxiliary training losses and rise mechanically when those losses are optimized, so as verdicts they systematically overstate physical competence (we measured reversals up to cosine $1.50\times$ better while nMSE degrades to $0.21\times$; our own early sweeps were misled this way before re-judging on prediction loss). nMSE has the opposite failure mode: its denominator degenerates when target variance collapses (a parabola ball exiting the frame drives late-horizon nMSE to $10^6$ while cosine stays normal), so parabola is judged on its clean \rmood{} partition and all numbers are reported per partition and per horizon. Full pathology catalogue and protocol checklist: Appendix~\ref{app:traps}. Splits are trajectory-level; three-seed results report mean$\pm$sample std.

\subsection{Injection Fails in 29 of 30 Cells}
\label{sec:scan}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig16_scan_paper.pdf}
\caption{Every injection variant $\times$ domain against the free-rollout baseline (nMSE ratio; $\dagger$ = three-seed mean, others single-seed). Vermillion = worse, white = parity, blue = better. Injection is at or below baseline in $29$ of $30$ cells ($25$ worse, $4$ parity); the collision column ($1.33$--$1.66\times$) is uniformly worst; the sole real gain is velocity$+$slot on parabola ($0.76\times$, $\dagger\checkmark$).}
\label{fig:scan}
\end{figure}

Injection degrades or fails to improve prediction in $29$ of $30$ mechanism--domain cells ($25$ worse, $4$ at parity; Figure~\ref{fig:scan}). Every cell carries the raw nMSE alongside the ratio, so the figure doubles as the full result table (seed annotations in Appendix~\ref{app:injection}); read family by family, it answers every design rationale of Table~\ref{tab:mechanisms}. \emph{Hard state injection}: the slot faithfully absorbs position (its supervision loss falls $2.53\!\to\!0.015$; slot decodability rises $0.31\!\to\!0.96$), yet prediction worsens in all three domains ($1.40\times$/$1.23\times$/$1.66\times$)---the constraint acts, harmfully. \emph{Soft injection}: the deep-supervision recipe, effective in a low-dimensional state latent where supervised quantities occupy ${\sim}38\%$ of the representation \citep{zahorodnii2025deepsup}, does not transfer to a $192$-D visual latent where they occupy $2\%$: all three domains degrade ($1.27\times$/parity-after-seeds/$1.65\times$), and combining soft with hard does not rescue ($1.54\times$ on collision). \emph{Correct physics does not help}: the strict $a{=}g$ head---the ground-truth functional form---degrades uniform by $1.57\times$ and parabola from $0.127$ to $0.173$, no better than an unconstrained MLP ($0.178$). \emph{The purpose-built design fails worst on its target}: the consistency loss, designed for collision impulses, is among the worst exactly there ($1.63\times$). \emph{Labels are not the issue}: the label-free prior and its perfectly labeled counterpart fail in the same direction (collision $1.66\times$ vs.\ $1.33\times$)---the failure is architectural, not informational.

The sole exception is the mechanism's signature, not a usable prior: adding \emph{velocity} to the re-weighted slot helps on parabola (\rmood{} $0.122{\pm}0.007\!\to\!0.096{\pm}0.007$, every seed lower) because there velocity is an extrapolable driving variable ($a{=}g$ makes it linear in time); the same encoding is the \emph{worst} variant on uniform ($+0.076$, velocity is a redundant constant) and clearly harmful on collision ($+0.025$ over the reweighted slot; velocity jumps discontinuously). A prior that requires knowing the domain's dynamics in advance, and reverses sign across domains, is a diagnostic, not a method---and its best case ($-0.026$ nMSE) is an order of magnitude below the protocol lever of \S\ref{sec:fr}.

\paragraph{From-scratch co-training hurts more.}
The remaining defense---structure must be baked in during pretraining---fails in the strongest way (Table~\ref{tab:scratch}): the physics penalty on uniform grows from $+0.035$ (fine-tuned) to $+0.558$ (from-scratch); all six regime--domain cells are positive. The cleaner the baseline, the larger the harm.

\paragraph{Two corroborations independent of PhyWorld nMSE.}
First, \emph{zero-shot transfer} (different dataset, metric, and task): on Physion OCP, no synthetic-trained configuration exceeds the random-encoder prior ($0.607$ mean AUC); the physics-structured variant is the \emph{worst} of all ($0.551$), below free rollout ($0.603$)---``more structure, worse transfer'' (Appendix~\ref{app:traps}). Second, \emph{realistic simulation}: trained directly on Physion++, every injection variant we ported (fixed slot, consistency, consistency$+$acceleration) degrades per-scenario rollout nMSE by $3$--$10\times$ against the identically trained free-rollout model (Table~\ref{tab:pp}); the gaps dwarf the three-seed noise band of the baseline. The probe family has not yet been run on Physion++, so this corroboration currently covers the slot and consistency families.

\subsection{Mechanism: Decodable but Not Load-Bearing}
\label{sec:mech}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.95\linewidth]{figures/fig1_thesis_presence_not_use.pdf}
\caption{Decodable, but not load-bearing (collision domain). The flat dashed line is position decodability from the frame embeddings (probe $\rho\approx0.84$): the state is \emph{present} at every horizon. Yet the teacher-forced rollout (LeWM's default) collapses from near-perfect one-step agreement ($0.99$) to $0.24$ by horizon $28$; training the same model by free rollout (\S\ref{sec:protocol}) stabilizes the long horizon ($0.48$) with no physics added. Presence of the state does not imply its use by prediction.}
\label{fig:presence}
\end{figure}

The puzzle of \S\ref{sec:intro} localizes here: position is linearly decodable from the latent at every horizon, yet the rollout drifts away from it (Figure~\ref{fig:presence})---\emph{decodable, but not load-bearing}. The three tests explain why.

\paragraph{Test 1: the physical constraint is injected.}
Probing \emph{only the $190$ non-slot dimensions} decodes position at $\rho=0.79$--$0.92$ across all three domains---as well as the full latent, undiminished by pinning the slot---while a random-2-dimension control fails ($0.2$--$0.5$). Position is a redundant, distributed copy the predictor can route through while ignoring any structured slot.

\paragraph{Test 2: the constraint acts harmfully.}
Both measurements move, which indicates the constraint acts. At probe weight $\lambda{=}10$ the weighted physical loss grows to $15$--$125\times$ the prediction loss (the gradient-magnitude proxy), and the encoder's effective dimensionality collapses by $39$--$90\%$; at $\lambda{=}1$, the setting of \S\ref{sec:scan}, the ratio is milder ($3$--$20\times$) yet the harm persists. With slot decodability rising $0.31\!\to\!0.96$, the constraint demonstrably reshapes the representation---in a direction that competes with prediction.

\paragraph{Test 3: re-weighting the physical loss does not help.}
Sweeping the slot weight $w{=}1\!\to\!300$ (Figure~\ref{fig:lbr}) moves the harm systematically---the mechanism's direction is confirmed---but weighting rescues only $2$ of $4$ domain--partition decision cells, to \emph{parity} and never a net gain: uniform both-OOD returns exactly to baseline ($0.132\pm0.017$ vs.\ $0.136\pm0.008$), uniform \rmood{} never recovers, and collision worsens monotonically with weight. Insufficient physical loss is therefore not the root cause: the black-box route stays open at every weight, and closing it requires changing the architecture.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig8_lbr_ablation.pdf}
\caption{Dose--response re-weighting. Left: uniform, slot weight $w{=}1\!\to\!300$ (the axis label pos\_weight~$\lambda$ denotes $w$) against the baseline band---both-OOD returns to parity, \rmood{} never does. \subsection{Free Rollout Reduces Error by $2.2$--$8.3\times$}
\label{sec:fr}

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figures/fig2_free_rollout_2models.pdf}
\caption{Teacher forcing vs.\ free rollout on both backbones (hardest clean split, nMSE $\downarrow$; fold-change above bars; all three-seed with non-overlapping intervals). Left, LeWM: uniform $0.300\!\to\!0.136$ ($2.2\times$), parabola $0.443\!\to\!0.122$ ($3.6\times$), collision $1.153\!\to\!0.479$ ($2.4\times$), Physion++ horizon-$64$ $1.174\!\to\!0.141$ ($8.3\times$). Right, the frozen-DINOv2 variant (\S\ref{sec:external}): the same physics-free switch yields $1.4\times$/$1.7\times$/$1.7\times$/$4.0\times$---the protocol lever is backbone-independent.}
\label{fig:fr}
\end{figure*}

Replacing teacher forcing with free rollout cuts the hardest-split error $2.2\times$ (uniform, $0.300{\pm}0.007\!\to\!0.136{\pm}0.008$), $3.6\times$ (parabola, $0.443{\pm}0.048\!\to\!0.122{\pm}0.007$), and $2.4\times$ (collision, $1.152{\pm}0.048\!\to\!0.479{\pm}0.079$), with improvements on \emph{every} partition including ID ($2.0$--$4.6\times$)---it is not an OOD patch (Figure~\ref{fig:fr}). On Physion++ the same switch is worth $8.3\times$ at horizon $64$ ($1.174{\pm}0.041\!\to\!0.141{\pm}0.018$, three seeds), and the entire pattern replicates on the frozen-DINOv2 backbone (Figure~\ref{fig:fr}, right; \S\ref{sec:externalres}). The horizon must match dynamics complexity: collision improves markedly at $H{\approx}20$ ($0.393\!\to\!0.294$) while smooth domains degrade with excess horizon (uniform horizon-$28$ cosine $0.969\!\to\!0.814$ at $H{=}16$); realistic-simulation scenes have the largest appetite---extending $H{=}8\!\to\!28$ (with geometric scale jitter stabilizing amplitude; Appendix~\ref{app:aug}) drives horizon-$64$ nMSE from $0.280$ to $0.014$, a ${\sim}19\times$ reduction with no inflection found (Figure~\ref{fig:ladder}).

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.95\linewidth]{figures/fig7_realdata_num_preds.pdf}
\caption{Physion++ direct training: per-horizon rollout nMSE (log scale) as the training horizon grows ($H{=}8\!\to\!28$, $+$scale jitter). Long-horizon error falls monotonically to $1/19$ of baseline with no inflection: realistic-simulation dynamics reward long-rollout training more than the synthetic domains do.}

\end{figure}

As the positive control of \S\ref{sec:protocol}, this result carries the methodological weight of the paper: on the same code, metrics, seeds, and data that judged all thirty injection cells, a physics-free intervention produces multiples---so the injection failure of \S\ref{sec:scan} is attributable to the injected structure, not to the pipeline or the yardstick.

\subsection{External Control: Extrinsic Is Necessary, Not Sufficient}
\label{sec:externalres}

\paragraph{Extrinsic port.}
The faithful extrinsic port (PIWM; \S\ref{sec:external}) learns the correct physics and, where its encoder is in-distribution, beats our shared-latent model: rolled-out position $\rho$ reaches $0.96$ (ID) and $0.97$ (\vood) vs.\ LeWM's $0.93$/$0.87$ (Table~\ref{tab:piwm}). But under radius/mass shift---which changes appearance, hitting the VAE encoder---it collapses to $\rho=0.33$ (\rmood) and $0.48$ (\bothood) while LeWM holds $0.89$/$0.87$. The architectural prediction of \S\ref{sec:mech} is confirmed from the outside: making the physical state load-bearing by construction is what extrinsic designs buy (their equations alone do not transfer, \S\ref{sec:scan}); yet closing the bypass leaves the encoder-OOD problem unsolved. Extrinsic structure is necessary but not sufficient.

\paragraph{Cross-backbone replication.}
On the frozen-DINOv2 variant every headline finding replicates across three seeds. Presence is even stronger---the physics-naive frozen encoder decodes position at $\rho{=}0.95$ (vs.\ $0.90$ for LeWM), with the same black-box bypass---so presence and bypass are properties of generic visual features, not our training. The protocol lever transfers ($1.4$--$1.7\times$ synthetic, $4.0\times$ Physion++; Figure~\ref{fig:fr}, right). Injection still never wins ($27/30$ at or below baseline; Appendix~\ref{app:crossbackbone}); the three above-parity cells dissolve under content-free controls---a shuffled target, or a $10\times$-weaker pin at matched loss weighting, keeps the apparent gain while ID error degrades, i.e.\ capacity-restriction regularization, not physics (Trap~5, Appendix~\ref{app:traps}). One difference: with the encoder frozen, injection is inert rather than harmful---the losses reshape only the adapter, bounding damage but foreclosing benefit. Across every control, the same sentence survives: physical state in a latent world model is \emph{decodable but not load-bearing}.
