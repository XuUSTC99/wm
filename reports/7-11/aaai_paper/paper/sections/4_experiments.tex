\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Model.}
The compact, fully controlled backbone of \S\ref{sec:prelim} (LeWM: $E_\theta$, an MLP projector, an action encoder, and a $6$-layer causal-transformer $F_\phi$) allows the complete factorial anatomy (${\sim}200$ training runs), with headline configurations repeated over three seeds ($3072/1234/42$); remaining cells are single-seed and marked as such.

\paragraph{Datasets and OOD protocol.}
All benchmarks are \emph{visual} world-modeling tasks (RGB video frames), not the numerical/tabular sequences of prior physics-injection work (\S\ref{sec:related}); injected state is thus always a small fraction of a high-dimensional visual latent. \emph{PhyWorld} \citep{kang2024how} provides three single-mechanism synthetic domains---\emph{uniform motion}, \emph{parabola}, \emph{collision} (zero/constant/impulsive acceleration)---each with $1{,}000$ ID trajectories ($32$ frames, $224{\times}224$; radius/mass $\in[0.7,1.5]$, velocity $\in[1,4]$), evaluated on four partitions (ID, \rmood{}, \vood{}, \bothood{}); its single-variable OOD ranges isolate physical extrapolation from data-scale confounds. Two \emph{realistic-simulation} benchmarks (rendered, not camera video) add realism: \emph{Physion} \citep{bear2021physion} for zero-shot object-contact-prediction (OCP) transfer, and \emph{Physion++} \citep{tung2023physionpp}---ten physical-property scenarios (mass/friction/bounce/deformation)---for direct training with rollout to horizon $64$.

\paragraph{Metrics and decision rules.}
Verdicts use two scale-aware, \emph{external} metrics---latent nMSE ($\|\hat{\lat}-\lat\|^2$ over target variance) and pixel PSNR of decoded rollouts---external because no injection variant optimizes them directly, and they agree in every case we examined. Latent cosine and linear-probe decodability $\rho$ \citep{alain2016probes} are \emph{diagnostics only}: both are duals of the auxiliary losses and rise mechanically when those are optimized, overstating physical competence (we measured cosine $1.50\times$ better while nMSE degraded to $0.21\times$). nMSE fails oppositely---its denominator degenerates when target variance collapses---so parabola is judged on its clean \rmood{} partition, and all numbers are reported per partition and per horizon. Full pathology catalogue: \appref{app:traps}{F}. Splits are trajectory-level; three-seed results report mean$\pm$sample std.

\subsection{Injection Fails in 29 of 30 Cells}
\label{sec:scan}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig16_scan_paper.pdf}
\caption{Every injection variant $\times$ domain against the free-rollout baseline (nMSE ratio; every cell and the baseline are three-seed means over seeds $3072/1234/42$). Warm = worse, pale = parity, blue = better. \emph{Hatched} cells are within noise---their three seeds overlap the baseline's, so the ratio alone overstates them; only solid cells separate from the baseline in every seed. Injection is at or below baseline in $29$ of $30$ cells ($17$ worse, $12$ within noise); the collision column is the warmest ($1.12$--$1.37\times$, no cell below baseline); the sole gain that separates from baseline in every seed is velocity$+$slot on parabola ($0.79\times$, the one solid blue cell). The two dynamics-head rows ($a{=}g$ and grounded) are the same configuration and print identical values (see text).}
\label{fig:scan}
\end{figure}

Injection degrades or fails to improve prediction in $29$ of $30$ mechanism--domain cells ($17$ worse in every seed, $12$ within noise; Figure~\ref{fig:scan}). Every cell carries the raw three-seed-mean nMSE alongside the ratio, so the figure doubles as the full result table (per-seed values in \appref{app:injection}{B}); read group by group, it answers every design rationale of Table~\ref{tab:mechanisms}. \emph{Hard state injection}: the slot faithfully absorbs position (its supervision loss falls $2.53\!\to\!0.015$; slot decodability rises $0.31\!\to\!0.96$), yet prediction never improves---worse on parabola and collision ($1.16\times$/$1.30\times$) and within noise on uniform ($1.19\times$): the constraint acts, and buys nothing. \emph{Soft injection}: the deep-supervision recipe, effective in a low-dimensional state latent where supervised quantities occupy ${\sim}38\%$ of the representation \citep{zahorodnii2025deepsup}, does not transfer to a $192$-D visual latent where they occupy $2\%$: it worsens the two both-OOD domains ($1.36\times$ uniform, $1.37\times$ collision) and is within noise on parabola, and combining soft with hard does not rescue ($1.30\times$ on collision). \emph{Correct physics does not help}: the strict $a{=}g$ head---the ground-truth functional form---worsens uniform by $1.27\times$ and parabola to $0.151$ nMSE, no better than an unconstrained MLP ($0.149$). \emph{The purpose-built design does not help its target}: the consistency loss, designed for collision impulses, still degrades collision ($1.27\times$). \emph{Labels are not the issue}: withholding ground truth changes nothing---the label-free prior and its labeled counterpart both fail to improve any domain in every seed (collision $1.34\times$ vs.\ $1.12\times$)---the failure is architectural, not informational.

The sole exception is the mechanism's signature, not a usable prior: adding \emph{velocity} to the re-weighted slot helps on parabola (\rmood{} $0.122{\pm}0.007\!\to\!0.096{\pm}0.007$, every seed lower) because there velocity is an extrapolable driving variable ($a{=}g$ makes it linear in time); the same encoding is harmful on uniform ($+0.049$ over baseline, velocity is a redundant constant) and on collision ($+0.017$ over the reweighted slot; velocity jumps discontinuously). A prior that requires knowing the domain's dynamics in advance, and reverses sign across domains, is a diagnostic, not a method---and its best case ($-0.026$ nMSE) is an order of magnitude below the protocol lever of \S\ref{sec:fr}.

\paragraph{From-scratch co-training does not rescue injection.}
The remaining defense---structure must be baked in during pretraining---does not hold (Table~\ref{tab:scratch}). Co-training from random initialization, over three seeds, leaves injection harmful on uniform ($+0.435$, Welch 95\% CI $[+0.276,+0.594]$) and collision ($+0.147$, $[+0.074,+0.220]$), and at parity on parabola ($-0.029$, $[-0.318,+0.260]$)---a domain whose from-scratch baseline is itself too seed-unstable ($0.343$--$0.666$ across seeds) to resolve an effect of that size. Parity is not a rescue: for this defense to hold, baking the structure in would have to make injection \emph{help}, and in no cell does it. Baking it in from the start also produces the single worst failure in the grid: on uniform the penalty is $+0.435$ from scratch against $+0.037$ fine-tuned, an order of magnitude larger. The two regimes trade places on the other two domains, so we do not read a general ordering between them---the robust statement is that neither escapes the harm.

\paragraph{Two corroborations independent of PhyWorld nMSE.}
First, \emph{zero-shot transfer} (different dataset, metric, and task): on Physion OCP, no synthetic-trained configuration exceeds the random-encoder prior ($0.607$ mean AUC over the eight scenarios); the physics-structured variant is the \emph{worst} of all ($0.551$), below free rollout ($0.603$), which approaches the prior monotonically over training ($0.554$ at epoch $1$ to $0.603$ at epoch $20$) without ever crossing it. Per scenario, only Support ($+0.10$) and Collide ($+0.07$) clear the random control at all---``more structure, worse transfer,'' and most of the apparent zero-shot competence is the architecture, not the learning (\appref{app:traps}{F}). Second, \emph{realistic simulation}: trained directly on Physion++, every injection variant we ported (fixed slot, consistency, consistency$+$acceleration) degrades per-scenario rollout nMSE by $3$--$10\times$ against the identically trained free-rollout model (\appref{app:injection}{B}); the gaps dwarf the three-seed noise band of the baseline. The probe variants have not yet been run on Physion++, so this corroboration covers the slot and consistency mechanisms only.

\subsection{Mechanism: Load-Bearing but Redundant}
\label{sec:mech}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig1_thesis_presence_not_use.pdf}
\caption{Decodable, used, and still drifting, in all three domains. Colour is the training protocol; line style is the domain. The shaded band is position decodability from the frame embeddings (\bothood{} probe $\rho$, mean over both coordinates): the state is \emph{present} to the same degree everywhere ($0.84$--$0.91$) and does not decay with horizon. The teacher-forced rollout nevertheless falls out of that band by an amount that tracks dynamics complexity---at horizon $28$ it reaches $0.84$ on uniform motion, $0.57$ on parabola and $0.24$ on collision, from $0.98$--$0.99$ at one step. Free rollout (\S\ref{sec:protocol}) recovers much of the gap with no physics added.}
\label{fig:presence}
\end{figure}

The puzzle of \S\ref{sec:intro} localizes here: in all three domains, position is linearly decodable from the latent at every horizon, yet the rollout drifts away from it, and the further the harder the dynamics (Figure~\ref{fig:presence}). Decodability alone would not settle what the model does with that state---probe-recoverable information need not be information the model uses \citep{elazar2021amnesic}---so we measure four things: that the redundant route \emph{exists} (Test 1), that the constraint \emph{acts} and competes with prediction (Test 2), that its strength is \emph{not} what limits it (Test 3), and, by intervening on trained models, which channel prediction actually reads (Test 4). Test 4 refutes the reading we first defended: the injected slot is not bypassed at all.

\paragraph{Test 1: the physical constraint is injected.}
Probing \emph{only the $190$ non-slot dimensions} decodes position as well as the full latent in all three domains, undiminished by pinning the slot, while a random-2-dimension control fails (Table~\ref{tab:mech}, top). Position is thus a redundant, distributed copy: a route exists that never passes through a structured slot. This establishes that the route is \emph{available}; that prediction takes it is the reading we defend below, not a measurement---linear decodability need not imply use \citep{elazar2021amnesic}, and the probe here reads frame embeddings, not rolled-out latents.

\paragraph{Test 2: the constraint acts harmfully.}
Both measurements move, which indicates the constraint acts (Table~\ref{tab:mech}, bottom): at probe weight $\lambda{=}10$ the weighted physical loss outweighs the prediction loss by one to two orders of magnitude (a gradient-magnitude proxy), and the encoder's effective dimensionality collapses with it. At $\lambda{=}1$, the setting of \S\ref{sec:scan}, the ratio is milder ($3$--$20\times$) yet the harm persists. The constraint demonstrably reshapes the representation---in a direction that competes with prediction.

\paragraph{Test 3: re-weighting the physical loss does not help.}
Sweeping the slot weight $w{=}1\!\to\!300$ across every partition (\appref{app:injection}{B}) moves the harm systematically---the mechanism's direction is confirmed---but weighting rescues only $2$ of $4$ domain--partition decision cells, to \emph{parity} and never a net gain: uniform both-OOD returns exactly to baseline ($0.132\pm0.017$ vs.\ $0.136\pm0.008$), uniform \rmood{} never recovers, and collision worsens monotonically with weight. Insufficient physical loss is therefore not the root cause. What the sweep cannot fix is what stays constant across it---the redundant route through the black box---which is why we read the limit as architectural, and why the extrinsic port of \S\ref{sec:external}, which removes that route by construction, is the informative test.

\paragraph{Test 4: the injected slot is load-bearing---and that is the point.}
Intervening on trained models overturns the natural reading of Tests 1--3. \emph{Steering}: offsetting the position the slot decodes moves the model's own black-box state by $1.5$--$2.0\times$ the offset, while a norm-matched random direction moves it by ${\approx}0$ ($-0.03$ to $+0.01$); the decomposition is additive to within $3\%$, so the perturbation stays in the linear regime. \emph{Counterfactual patching}: replacing the slot with a donor trajectory's makes prediction follow the donor $47$--$72\%$ of the way, where the same operation on a model with no injected slot---two dimensions of no physical meaning---moves it $1.5$--$11\%$. \emph{Jacobians}: differentiating the predicted position with respect to each latent coordinate, no intervention designed, ranks the two injected dimensions $1$st and $2$nd of $192$ by sensitivity, against ranks ${\sim}99$ for the same two dimensions before injection. Three methods with disjoint failure modes agree, over three seeds and both backbones (\appref{app:causal}{D}).

So prediction does not route around the slot; it reads it. What the same interventions also show is that the black box keeps its own copy: pinning the slot leaves black-box decodability undiminished (Table~\ref{tab:mech}), and the black box still holds $96$--$99\%$ of total Jacobian sensitivity by sheer dimension count. Both channels are load-bearing, and prediction error does not move---which is what the redundancy hypothesis predicts and what a starved slot would not. The injected slot is \emph{load-bearing but redundant}: it carries load, and the load it carries is a duplicate.

Replacing teacher forcing with free rollout cuts the hardest-split error $2.2$--$3.6\times$ across the three domains and $8.3\times$ on Physion++, every teacher-forcing/free-rollout interval disjoint over three seeds, and the whole pattern replicates on the frozen-DINOv2 backbone (Figure~\ref{fig:fr}; per-domain values in \appref{app:crossbackbone}{C}). Crucially the gain is not an OOD patch: \emph{every} partition improves, including ID ($2.0$--$4.6\times$). The horizon must match dynamics complexity: collision improves markedly at $H{\approx}20$ ($0.393\!\to\!0.294$) while smooth domains degrade with excess horizon (uniform horizon-$28$ cosine $0.969\!\to\!0.814$ at $H{=}16$); realistic-simulation scenes have the largest appetite---extending $H{=}8\!\to\!28$ (with geometric scale jitter stabilizing amplitude; \appref{app:aug}{E}) drives horizon-$64$ nMSE from $0.280$ to $0.014$, a ${\sim}19\times$ reduction with no inflection found (\appref{app:aug}{E}).

As the positive control of \S\ref{sec:protocol}, this result carries the methodological weight of the paper: on the same code, metrics, seeds, and data that judged all thirty injection cells, a physics-free intervention produces multiples---so the injection failure of \S\ref{sec:scan} is attributable to the injected structure, not to the pipeline or the yardstick.

\subsection{External Control: Extrinsic Is Necessary, Not Sufficient}
\label{sec:externalres}

\paragraph{Extrinsic port.}
The faithful extrinsic port (PIWM; \S\ref{sec:external}) learns the correct physics and, where its encoder is in-distribution, beats our shared-latent model: rolled-out position $\rho$ reaches $0.96$ (ID) and $0.97$ (\vood) vs.\ LeWM's $0.93$/$0.87$. But under radius/mass shift---which changes appearance, hitting the VAE encoder---it collapses to $\rho=0.33$ (\rmood) and $0.48$ (\bothood) while LeWM holds $0.89$/$0.87$; appearance-changing shift is an axis its original case studies do not vary. The architectural prediction of \S\ref{sec:mech} is confirmed from the outside: making the physical state load-bearing by construction is what extrinsic designs buy (their equations alone do not transfer, \S\ref{sec:scan}); yet closing the bypass leaves the encoder-OOD problem unsolved. Extrinsic structure is necessary but not sufficient.

\paragraph{Cross-backbone replication.}
On the frozen-DINOv2 variant every headline finding replicates across three seeds. Presence is even stronger---the physics-naive frozen encoder decodes position at $\rho{=}0.95$ (vs.\ $0.90$ for LeWM), with the same redundant black-box copy---so both the presence of the state and its duplication across the latent are properties of generic visual features, not of our training. The protocol lever transfers ($1.4$--$1.7\times$ synthetic, $4.0\times$ Physion++; hatched bars in Figure~\ref{fig:fr}). Injection still never wins ($27/30$ at or below baseline; \appref{app:crossbackbone}{C}); the three above-parity cells dissolve under content-free controls---a shuffled target, or a $10\times$-weaker pin at matched loss weighting, keeps the apparent gain while ID error degrades, i.e.\ capacity-restriction regularization, not physics (Trap~5, \appref{app:traps}{F}). One difference: with the encoder frozen, injection is inert rather than harmful---the losses reshape only the adapter, bounding damage but foreclosing benefit. Across every control, the same sentence survives: physical state in a latent world model is \emph{load-bearing but redundant}.
