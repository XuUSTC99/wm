\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Model.}
The compact backbone of \S\ref{sec:prelim} allows the complete factorial anatomy (${\sim}200$ training runs). Every primary result---the $27$-cell scan and its baselines, the re-weighting sweep, the training-regime $2{\times}2$, and the Physion++ injection table---is three-seed ($3072/1234/42$).

\paragraph{Datasets and OOD protocol.}
All benchmarks are \emph{visual} world-modeling tasks (RGB video), not the numerical/tabular sequences of prior physics-injection work (\S\ref{sec:related}), so injected state is always a small fraction of a high-dimensional visual latent. \emph{PhyWorld} \citep{kang2025how} provides three single-mechanism domains---\emph{uniform motion}, \emph{parabola}, \emph{collision} (zero/constant/impulsive acceleration)---each with $1{,}000$ ID trajectories, evaluated on four partitions (ID, \rmood{}, \vood{}, \bothood{}) whose single-variable OOD ranges isolate physical extrapolation. Two \emph{realistic-simulation} benchmarks (rendered, not camera video) add realism: \emph{Physion} \citep{bear2021physion} for zero-shot object-contact prediction, and \emph{Physion++} \citep{tung2023physionpp} (ten physical-property scenarios) for direct training with rollout to horizon $64$.

\paragraph{Metrics and decision rules.}
Verdicts use two scale-aware, \emph{external} metrics---latent nMSE ($\|\hat{\lat}-\lat\|^2$ over target variance) and pixel PSNR of decoded rollouts---external because no injection variant optimizes them directly, and they agree in every case we examined. Latent cosine and probe decodability $\rho$ \citep{alain2016probes} are \emph{diagnostics only}: both are duals of the auxiliary losses and rise mechanically when those are optimized. nMSE fails oppositely---its denominator degenerates when target variance collapses---so parabola is judged on its clean \rmood{} partition (pathology catalogue: \appref{app:traps}{F}). Splits are trajectory-level; three-seed results report mean$\pm$sample std.

\subsection{Injection Fails in 26 of 27 Cells}
\label{sec:scan}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig16_scan_paper.pdf}
\caption{Every injection variant $\times$ domain vs.\ the free-rollout baseline (nMSE ratio; every cell and the baseline are three-seed means). Warm = worse, blue = better; $^{\circ}$ = within noise (the cell's seeds overlap the baseline's). Injection fails to improve on baseline in $26$ of $27$ cells ($15$ worse, $11$ within noise); the sole seed-robust gain is velocity$+$slot on parabola ($0.79\times$). Strict $a{=}g$ and the grounded prior are one configuration launched twice; the merged row pools all four draws.}
\label{fig:scan}
\end{figure}

Injection degrades or fails to improve prediction in $26$ of $27$ mechanism--domain cells ($15$ worse in every seed, $11$ within noise; Figure~\ref{fig:scan}, per-seed values in \appref{app:injection}{B}). Read group by group, the scan answers every design rationale of Table~\ref{tab:mechanisms}. \emph{Hard state injection}: the slot faithfully absorbs position (supervision loss $2.53\!\to\!0.015$; slot decodability $0.31\!\to\!0.96$), yet prediction never improves ($1.16$--$1.30\times$ worse or within noise): the constraint acts, and buys nothing. \emph{Soft injection}: the deep-supervision recipe, effective in a low-dimensional ($8$-D) state latent \citep{zahorodnii2025deepsup}, does not transfer to a $192$-D visual latent where the injected state is $2$ of $192$ dimensions ($1.36$--$1.37\times$ on the both-OOD domains), and combining soft with hard does not rescue. \emph{Correct physics does not help}: the strict $a{=}g$ head worsens uniform by $1.27\times$ and matches a freely learned acceleration on parabola ($0.151$ vs.\ $0.149$). \emph{The purpose-built design does not help its target}: the consistency loss, designed for collision impulses, still degrades collision ($1.27\times$). \emph{Labels are not the issue}: the label-free prior and its labeled control---the $a{=}g$ row, the identical specification with ground truth added---fail alike (collision $1.34\times$ vs.\ $1.12\times$): the failure is architectural, not informational.

The sole exception is the mechanism's signature, not a usable prior: adding \emph{velocity} to the re-weighted slot helps on parabola ($0.122{\pm}0.007\!\to\!0.096{\pm}0.007$, every seed lower) because there velocity is the extrapolable driving variable; the same encoding is harmful on uniform (velocity is a redundant constant) and collision (velocity jumps discontinuously). A prior that requires knowing each domain's dynamics in advance, and reverses sign across domains, is a diagnostic, not a method---and its best case is an order of magnitude below the protocol lever of \S\ref{sec:fr}.

\paragraph{From-scratch co-training does not rescue injection.}
The remaining defense---structure must be baked in during pretraining---does not hold (Table~\ref{tab:scratch}). Over three seeds, co-training from random initialization leaves injection harmful on uniform ($+0.435$, Welch 95\% CI $[+0.276,+0.594]$) and collision ($+0.147$, $[+0.074,+0.220]$), and at parity on parabola ($-0.029$, $[-0.318,+0.260]$; its from-scratch baseline is too seed-unstable to resolve an effect this small). Parity is not a rescue---the defense requires injection to \emph{help}, and in no cell does it; baking the structure in from the start even produces the single worst failure in the grid ($+0.435$ on uniform, vs.\ $+0.037$ fine-tuned).

\paragraph{Two corroborations independent of PhyWorld nMSE.}
First, \emph{zero-shot transfer}: on Physion OCP, no synthetic-trained configuration exceeds the random-encoder prior ($0.607$ mean AUC), and the physics-structured variant is the \emph{worst} of all ($0.551$, below free rollout's $0.603$)---``more structure, worse transfer,'' with most apparent zero-shot competence owed to the architecture, not the learning (\appref{app:traps}{F}). Second, \emph{realistic simulation}: trained directly on Physion++ over three seeds, every injection variant we ported---across all three mechanism families (slot, consistency, and the deep-supervision probe)---degrades rollout nMSE $3.6$--$11.9\times$ against the identically trained free-rollout model, worse in \emph{every} seed on all three mass scenarios (the fourth is too baseline-unstable to resolve; \appref{app:injection}{B}).

\subsection{Mechanism: Load-Bearing but Redundant}
\label{sec:mech}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig1_thesis_presence_not_use.pdf}
\caption{Decodable, yet drifting---one panel per domain. In each, the shaded band is that domain's position decodability from frame embeddings (\bothood{} probe $\rho$, per coordinate): high everywhere ($0.80$--$0.96$), no decay with horizon. The teacher-forced rollout (orange) falls out of the band by an amount tracking dynamics complexity (horizon-$28$ latent cosine: $0.84$ uniform, $0.57$ parabola, $0.24$ collision, from $0.98$--$0.99$ at one step); free rollout (blue) recovers much of the gap with no physics added.}
\label{fig:presence}
\end{figure}

The puzzle of \S\ref{sec:intro} localizes here: position is linearly decodable at every horizon, yet the rollout drifts away from it, the further the harder the dynamics (Figure~\ref{fig:presence}). Decodability alone cannot settle what the model \emph{does} with that state \citep{elazar2021amnesic}. We therefore conduct four tests to uncover the effect and mechanism of physical injection. Table~\ref{tab:mech} carries the crux on two axes: the state is \emph{already} in the latent, redundantly (top), so an injected copy is a duplicate; yet the constraint that writes it dominates the optimization and collapses the encoder (bottom). No new signal, real cost---the two reasons injection cannot help, before any intervention.

\paragraph{Test 1: the physical constraint is injected but redundant.}
Probing \emph{only the $190$ non-slot dimensions} decodes position as well as the full latent in all three domains, undiminished by pinning the slot, while a random-2-dimension control fails (Table~\ref{tab:mech}, top). Thus, the position is injected but a redundant copy.

\paragraph{Test 2: the redundant constraint acts harmfully.}
Both measurements move (Table~\ref{tab:mech}, bottom): at $\lambda{=}10$ the weighted physical loss outweighs the prediction loss by one to two orders of magnitude, and the encoder's effective dimensionality collapses with it; at $\lambda{=}1$, the setting of \S\ref{sec:scan}, the ratio is milder ($3$--$21\times$) yet the harm still persists.

\paragraph{Test 3: re-weighting the physical loss does not help.}
Sweeping $w{=}1\!\to\!300$ (\appref{app:injection}{B}) moves the harm systematically, but weighting rescues only $2$ of $4$ decision cells, to \emph{parity} and never a net gain: uniform both-OOD returns exactly to baseline ($0.132{\pm}0.017$ vs.\ $0.136{\pm}0.008$), uniform \rmood{} never recovers, collision worsens monotonically. Thus, insufficient physical loss is not the root cause.

\paragraph{Test 4: the injected slot is load-bearing.}
We test the slot's causal role three disjoint ways---two interventions and a non-interventional check---to examine whether the injected slot is load-bearing (three seeds, both backbones; excluded cells and full protocol: \appref{app:causal}{D}). \emph{Steering} the position the slot decodes moves the model's own black-box state by $1.5$--$2.0\times$ the offset, while a norm-matched random direction moves it ${\approx}0$ (additive to within $3\%$, so the perturbation stays linear). \emph{Patching} the slot with a donor trajectory's makes prediction follow the donor $47$--$72\%$ of the way, against $1.5$--$11\%$ for the same operation on a model with no injected slot. \emph{Jacobians} of predicted position w.r.t.\ each latent coordinate---which perturb nothing---rank the two injected dimensions $1$--$2$ of $192$, up from ${\sim}99$ without injection. All three agree: the injected slot is load-bearing---prediction reads it and depends on it causally.

Together, the four tests establish that the injected slot is load-bearing but redundant: it is written into the latent (Test 1) yet duplicates state the black box already carries, so it competes for capacity and gradient without adding new signal (Tests 2–3); the injection sits on the prediction pathway (Test 4), where a mis-specified constraint degrades performance while a correct one yields no gain.

\subsection{Free Rollout Reduces Error by $2.2$--$8.3\times$}
\label{sec:fr}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/fig2_free_rollout_2models.pdf}
\caption{Teacher forcing vs.\ free rollout, both backbones, grouped by domain---uniform motion, parabola, collision, Physion++ (hardest clean split; nMSE $\downarrow$; r/m $=$ radius/mass-OOD split, h64 $=$ horizon $64$; solid = LeWM, hatched = frozen DINOv2; blue = free rollout, orange = teacher forcing; three-seed means $\pm$ std, every interval disjoint; per-bar values in \appref{app:crossbackbone}{C}). Free rollout is worth $2.2$--$8.3\times$ on LeWM and $1.4$--$4.0\times$ on frozen DINOv2---backbone-independent, largest where dynamics are hardest.}
\label{fig:fr}
\end{figure}

Replacing teacher forcing with free rollout---a training-protocol change that injects no physics---cuts the hardest-split error $2.2$--$3.6\times$ across the three domains and $8.3\times$ on Physion++, every interval disjoint over three seeds, and the whole pattern replicates on frozen DINOv2 (Figure~\ref{fig:fr}; \appref{app:crossbackbone}{C}). The gain is not an OOD patch: \emph{every} partition improves, including ID ($2.0$--$4.6\times$). The horizon must match dynamics complexity---collision improves markedly at $H{\approx}20$ while smooth domains degrade with excess horizon, and realistic-simulation scenes benefit most from a longer horizon: $H{=}8\!\to\!28$ drives horizon-$64$ nMSE from $0.280$ to $0.014$, ${\sim}19\times$ (\appref{app:aug}{E}).

As the positive control of \S\ref{sec:protocol}, this carries the paper's methodological weight: on the same code, metrics, seeds, and data that judged all $27$ cells, a physics-free intervention produces $2.2$--$8.3\times$ gains---so the injection failure is attributable to the structure, not the pipeline or the yardstick.

\subsection{External Control: Removing the Redundancy Is Not Enough}
\label{sec:externalres}

\paragraph{Extrinsic port.}
The faithful extrinsic port (PIWM; \S\ref{sec:external}) learns the correct physics and, where its encoder is in-distribution, edges out our shared-latent model ($\rho$ $0.96$/$0.97$ vs.\ $0.93$/$0.87$). But under radius/mass shift---which changes appearance, hitting the VAE encoder---it collapses to $\rho{=}0.33$/$0.48$ while LeWM holds $0.89$/$0.87$. This is consistent with \S\ref{sec:mech} from the outside: making the physical state the \emph{sole, non-redundant} carrier buys a small in-distribution gain that injection into a shared latent never did (their equations alone do not transfer, \S\ref{sec:scan})---but it concentrates prediction on one brittle encoder, so removing the redundancy trades the duplicated-state problem for an encoder-OOD one instead of solving it. An extrinsic sole-carrier design is not, by itself, a fix.

\paragraph{Cross-backbone replication.}
On the frozen-DINOv2 variant every primary finding replicates across three seeds. Presence is even stronger---the physics-naive frozen encoder decodes position at $\rho{=}0.95$ (vs.\ $0.90$ for LeWM), with the same redundant black-box copy---so both the state and its duplication are properties of generic visual features, not of our training. The protocol lever transfers ($1.4$--$4.0\times$; Figure~\ref{fig:fr}); injection still never wins ($26$ of $27$ cells fail to improve on baseline; on this backbone the one cell below $1.0$ reaches only $0.97\times$, a $0.001$ nMSE gap within seed noise), and the apparent uniform gains dissolve under content-free controls into capacity-restriction regularization, not physics (Trap~5, \appref{app:traps}{F}). One difference: with the encoder frozen, injection is largely inert rather than harmful---the losses reshape only the adapter, bounding damage but foreclosing benefit. Across every control the same sentence survives: physical state in a latent world model is \emph{load-bearing but redundant}.
