\section{Method: A Design Space of Physical Injection}
\label{sec:method}

We answer the question of \S\ref{sec:intro} by construction: design the ways physics can be injected into a shared latent, state what each design is supposed to achieve, and lay out the experiments that test the mechanism behind it.

\subsection{Setting and Notation}
\label{sec:prelim}

We study LeWM \citep{maes2025lewm}, a compact JEPA-style action-conditioned world model (${\sim}10$M parameters): an encoder $E_\theta$ (ViT-tiny) maps frame $o_t$ to a latent $z_t\in\mathbb{R}^{D}$ with $D{=}192$, and a causal-transformer predictor $F_\phi$ rolls latents forward from the history and action $u_t$, i.e.\ $\hat z_{t+1}=F_\phi(z_{\le t},u_t)$. Training minimizes a prediction loss, an anti-collapse regularizer \citep{balestriero2025lejepa}, and---when physics is injected---an auxiliary physical loss:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{pred}}+\beta\,\mathrm{SIGReg}(z)+\lambda\,\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{phys}}.
\label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
Here $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{pred}}=\frac{1}{D}\sum_i w_i(\hat z_{t+1,i}-z_{t+1,i})^2$ with $w_i{=}1$ by default; \emph{re-weighting} raises $w_i$ to $w$ on the $2$ slot dimensions ($w$ swept $1\!\to\!300$), while $\lambda$ scales the soft auxiliary losses---two distinct dials. \emph{Teacher forcing} trains $F_\phi$ on one-step transitions from encoded ground-truth context; \emph{free rollout} feeds predictions back for $H$ autoregressive steps ($H{=}8$ unless stated) and supervises the whole rollout. The kinematic head (Group~2 below) replaces the slot's transition by a second-order update
\begin{equation}
\hat z^{[0:2]}_{t+1}=z^{[0:2]}_{t}+v_t\,\Delta t+\tfrac{1}{2}\,a\,\Delta t^{2},
\label{eq:kin}
\end{equation}
where $v_t$ is the finite-difference slot velocity and the acceleration $a$ is either a learned $\mathrm{MLP}(z_t,u_t)$ or a single learnable constant---the strict $a{=}g$ head. Unless stated otherwise the encoder is initialized from the public PushT checkpoint; \S\ref{sec:scan} additionally studies from-scratch training.

\subsection{Injection Design Space: Three Groups, Nine Variants}
\label{sec:designspace}

We study one family: \emph{intrinsic coordinate injection}---physics written onto the coordinates of a latent the predictor already shares, leaving the encoder $E_\theta$ and predictor $F_\phi$ otherwise untouched. This is the family targeted by the familiar ``just add a physics term'' prescription; within it we vary three orthogonal axes: injecting \emph{what the motion state is} vs.\ \emph{how it evolves}, under \emph{hard vs.\ soft} constraints, with \emph{labeled vs.\ label-free} supervision (Table~\ref{tab:mechanisms}).

Two families lie outside it and our results do not speak to them: designs that change what the representation \emph{is} (object-centric slots, relational graphs; \citealp{locatello2020slot,battaglia2016interaction,sanchez2020gns}) and designs that replace the predictor's functional form---the \emph{transition operator} (Hamiltonian/symplectic integrators, learned contact; \citealp{cranmer2020lnn}). Both alter the architecture rather than annotate its coordinates---where \S\ref{sec:mech} argues the leverage lies; we test one as an external control (\S\ref{sec:external}).

Orthogonally, we cross injection with the \emph{training regime}---fine-tuning a pretrained encoder vs.\ from-scratch co-training, injection on/off, a $2{\times}2$ per domain. This targets the objection that a constraint grafted onto a settled representation never gets to shape it: co-training from random initialization puts the constraint in from the first gradient step, so for the objection to hold, injection would have to help---or at least stop hurting---in that regime (\S\ref{sec:scan}).

\subsection{Mechanistic Hypothesis and Its Tests}
\label{sec:mechtests}

Two properties are easy to conflate, and separating them is what the tests below are for. A channel is \textbf{load-bearing} if perturbing it changes the prediction---causal, measurable only by intervention. A channel is \textbf{marginal} if it supplies information the rest of the latent does not---informational, visible in whether adding it improves prediction. Probing establishes neither \citep{elazar2021amnesic}; the two come apart exactly when a representation is redundant, which is our case.

\emph{Hypothesis (the redundancy problem).} The physical slot occupies $2$ of $192$ dimensions, while the black-box channel \emph{redundantly} encodes the same position---so an injected slot duplicates what the latent already carries. Prediction may well read the injected copy (\S\ref{sec:mech} shows it does), but reading a duplicate cannot supply information the representation lacks: injection adds no new signal while still consuming capacity and gradient share. We test this account in four ways, each admitting a specific outcome that would falsify it:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Is the physical constraint injected?} Split the latent into the $2$ slot dimensions and the $190$ black-box dimensions and decode position from each separately, with a random-2-dimension control \citep{hewitt2019control}. If the black box alone cannot decode position, the redundancy claim is false.
    \item \textbf{Is the constraint acting at all?} Measure the weighted physical loss relative to the prediction loss (a gradient-magnitude proxy) and the encoder's participation ratio. If neither moves, the failure is a bug; if the term dominates and dimensionality collapses, the constraint is acting---and competing with prediction.
    \item \textbf{Does re-weighting help?} Sweep the slot weight $w$ from $1$ to $300$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:loss}). If harm does not respond to the weight, the gradient-share mechanism is wrong; if it responds but saturation still cannot rescue every domain, insufficient physical loss is not the root cause.
    \item \textbf{Is the injected slot load-bearing?} Tests 1--3 never contain the variable that matters here---which channel prediction reads. We therefore intervene on trained models three ways: \emph{steering} (offset the position one channel decodes, read the change off the \emph{other}, against a norm-matched random direction), \emph{counterfactual patching} (replace a channel with a donor trajectory's values, measure how far the rollout follows the donor, against a model with no slot to replace), and \emph{per-dimension Jacobians} (differentiate predicted position w.r.t.\ each latent coordinate---no intervention designed). If the slot bears no load, all three read zero on it.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{The Training-Protocol Control: Free Rollout}
\label{sec:protocol}

Free rollout serves two methodological roles; neither is a novelty claim (it is scheduled sampling, \citealp{bengio2015scheduled}).

\emph{The training protocol as a controlled factor.} Physical structure has never been compared against the training protocol on the same baseline; prior studies treat the protocol as background. We set the protocol first---replacing teacher forcing with free rollout---then ask whether structure adds incremental value. Teacher forcing hides compounding error at training time (\emph{exposure bias}); free rollout exposes it, injecting no physics. But the gain is more than robustness: supervising an $H$-step rollout grades the predictor on \emph{evolving} the state over the horizon rather than reproducing one-step transitions---an evolution-targeted training signal needing no physics labels, training \emph{how the state evolves} without touching \emph{what the state is}. The horizon $H$ must match dynamics complexity: domains with late causal events need horizons long enough to contain them.

\emph{Positive control.} A negative result invites two deflations---``your implementation is broken'' or ``your metrics cannot detect improvement.'' A protocol change producing large gains on identical code and metrics rules out both, pinning the failure on the injected structure itself.

\subsection{External and Cross-Backbone Controls}
\label{sec:external}

Two controls probe our account from outside the LeWM backbone: one tests whether an architecture that \emph{removes} the redundancy succeeds where injection fails, the other whether the conclusions survive a different encoder.

\emph{Extrinsic port.} We faithfully port the physically interpretable world model (PIWM) of \citet{mao2024piwm}---VAE encoder, supervised extractor to a low-dimensional physical state, known-equation dynamics---onto the same domains and OOD protocol. Extrinsic designs make the physical state the \emph{sole} carrier, removing the redundancy by construction; the port tests that prediction from the outside.

\emph{Cross-backbone control.} We replicate the core protocol on a second JEPA-family instantiation in the style of DINO-WM \citep{zhou2025dinowm}: a \emph{frozen} DINOv2-small encoder \citep{oquab2024dinov2} that has never seen PhyWorld, the trainable projector as adapter, and the identical predictor stack, losses, seeds, and evaluation---differing from LeWM in backbone provenance, dimensionality, and in whether the injected losses can reshape the encoder at all.
